# Process-Centered Assessment Redesign · entornos-digitales-digital-innovation-studio-2026

[Versión en español](before-after.es.md)

> This report contains concise, evidence-linked design rationales, not hidden model reasoning.

## Why the current assessment needs redesign

The rubric claims to assess critical reasoning, evidence, iteration, and responsible AI use, but almost all graded evidence appears in final team products. A polished AI-assisted submission can therefore score well without establishing how students framed the problem, rejected alternatives, improved from feedback, verified AI output, or contributed individually.

### Learning processes that were previously invisible

- Problem framing before solution selection
- Evidence used to reject alternatives
- Iteration from baseline to improved prototype
- Response to peer or faculty feedback
- Individual ownership of consequential decisions
- Verification and rejection of AI-generated suggestions

## Before and after

| Evidence model | Before | Approved redesign |
|---|---:|---:|
| Final product | 95% | 40.0% |
| Process checkpoints (excluding individual verification) | 0% | 55.0% |
| Individual verification | 5% | 5.0% |
| Total non-final evidence | 5% | 60.0% |

Selected option: **Balanced studio evidence**

Faculty workload: Moderate: three concise graded milestones, formative checkpoints, and a short individual questionnaire.

Trade-offs:
- Produces stronger longitudinal evidence but requires scheduled checkpoint review.
- Reduces final-product weight while preserving the authentic dossier and Demo Day.

## Confirmed faculty decisions

- **FD-01**: Move 30% of the course grade into three evidence-based milestones worth 10% each. — The milestones make framing, design decisions, validation, and governance visible before the final product.
- **FD-02**: Retain the authentic final dossier/prototype at 40% and Demo Day defense at 25%. — The final product remains important, but no longer carries the entire validity burden.
- **FD-03**: Add a 5% individual post-Demo-Day questionnaire. — A short individual instrument is feasible for the cohort and establishes contribution, decision ownership, learning, and responsible AI use.
- **FD-04**: Require disclosure and verification of consequential AI use across the project. — Responsible AI use must be evidenced through decisions and verification, not only a generic declaration.
- **FD-05**: Add formative checkpoints that require teams to respond to feedback. — Feedback should change later work and create observable evidence of iteration without adding more summative grading.

## Adversarial validity checks

### ADV-01 · A team uses generative AI to produce a polished dossier and prototype but cannot explain the key choices.

- Risk: The current final-product rubric could reward quality without establishing the intended reasoning.
- Mitigation: Use graded milestones, decision evidence, verification records, and the individual questionnaire before accepting the final artifact as evidence.

### ADV-02 · One student performs most consequential work while the whole team presents the result.

- Risk: Team artifacts and Demo Day cannot establish individual mastery or contribution.
- Mitigation: Require each student to identify a consequential decision, rejected alternative, supporting evidence, and verifiable contribution in the individual questionnaire.

### ADV-03 · A team demonstrates strong inquiry and improvement but the final prototype remains imperfect.

- Risk: A product-dominant grade can underrepresent meaningful learning and evidence-based iteration.
- Mitigation: Allocate 30% to milestones and require explicit feedback response so process quality has independent weight.
